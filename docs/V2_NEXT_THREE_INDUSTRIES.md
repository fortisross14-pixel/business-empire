# Business Empire v2.0 — Recommended Next Three Industries

The best first expansion set is **Packaged Food**, **Apparel**, and **Consumer Electronics**.
Together they stress three different business models—repeat-purchase/expiry, fashion/seasonality,
and high-cost innovation/reliability—while reusing the existing product, segment, IP, channel,
storage and manufacturing foundations.

## 1. Packaged Food

### Strategic identity

High repeat purchase, brutal shelf competition and strong value pressure. Taste can produce
volume, health can command a premium, and children's products can be transformed by IP. Margin
is sensitive to retailer cuts, commodity costs, spoilage and production scale.

Core priorities: **Taste, Health, Convenience, Value, Natural, Licensed/IP**.

| Product | Commercial character | Strongest impacts |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Breakfast Cereal | Large family market; retailer-led; strong repeat purchase | Taste ★★★★★, Value ★★★★, Health ★★★, IP ★★★★★ for children; cardboard/grain/sugar costs; long shelf life |
| Snack Bars | Smaller packs and higher price per unit | Convenience ★★★★★, Health ★★★★, Natural ★★★★, Taste ★★★; professional/active audiences; premium margin possible |
| Potato Chips | Broad impulse product with heavy promotion | Taste ★★★★★, Value ★★★★, Social ★★★★; commodity/oil exposure; high volume, moderate shelf life |
| Cookies & Biscuits | Family sharing plus premium gifting niche | Taste ★★★★★, Value ★★★, Natural ★★; IP ★★★ for children; premium packaging can support affluent buyers |
| Candy | Volatile hits, impulse channels and licensing | Taste ★★★★★, IP ★★★★★, Novelty/Collectible ★★★★, Value ★★★; fad risk and seasonal peaks |
| Ready-to-Eat Meals | Convenience-led but operationally harder | Convenience ★★★★★, Taste ★★★★, Health ★★★; refrigerated storage, shorter shelf life and logistics risk |

### Segment preferences by product

| Segment | Best products | What they reward | What loses them |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Budget families | Cereal, chips, cookies | Low unit price, large pack, familiar taste, relevant children's IP | Premium ingredients without visible value |
| Affluent families | Cereal, bars, premium cookies | Health, natural ingredients, clean packaging, trust | Excess sugar and discount presentation |
| Urban professionals | Snack bars, ready meals | Convenience, portability, health, online/subscription | Family-size formats and weak delivery coverage |
| Young trend seekers | Chips, candy | Bold flavors, novelty, social buzz, collectible/IP drops | Safe evergreen propositions |
| Health-focused adults | Bars, cereal, ready meals | Nutrition, natural ingredients, scientific claims, trust | Empty licensing and indulgence-only positioning |

### Required icons

`cereal.svg`, `snackbar.svg`, `chips.svg`, `cookies.svg`, `candy.svg`, `readymeal.svg`.
Each should use the existing 512×360 transparent-compatible SVG product-card composition:
recognizable package plus product, three-quarter view, soft contact shadow, bright retail color,
no embedded brand text, and enough detail to remain legible at 80 px.

### Special building — Food Innovation Kitchen

- **Gameplay:** unlocks AA food work at level 1; level 2 improves sensory-testing speed,
  shelf-life validation and food-project capacity. Reduces recall/spoilage risk but never grants
  demand directly.
- **Qualities:** formulation, sensory testing, nutrition, shelf-life validation, pilot batches.
- **Map asset:** `food_innovation_kitchen.png`, 768×768 RGBA, same isometric camera and footprint
  as Beauty/Toy Centers. Warm brick and cream shell, colorful test kitchen windows, rooftop vents,
  small herb planters and a visible tasting/pilot line. No signage text baked into the art.

## 2. Apparel

### Strategic identity

Fast cycles, seasonality and brand meaning. Basic garments compete on value and durability;
fashion-led collections win through style and speed; luxury accessories can earn exceptional
margin at tiny volume. IP matters strongly for graphic products and children.

Core priorities: **Style, Comfort, Durability, Value, Sustainability, Luxury/Prestige, Licensed/IP**.

| Product | Commercial character | Strongest impacts |
| --- | --- | --- |
| T-Shirt | Low entry cost and broad reach | Value ★★★★★, Style ★★★★, Comfort ★★★; IP ★★★★★ for graphics; cotton-price exposure |
| Hoodie | Higher ticket, seasonal and youth-skewed | Comfort ★★★★★, Style ★★★★, Durability ★★★; IP ★★★★; winter seasonality |
| Denim | Evergreen but fit/quality sensitive | Durability ★★★★★, Comfort ★★★★, Style ★★★★; repeat purchase low; returns risk |
| Activewear | Performance-premium opportunity | Comfort ★★★★★, Performance ★★★★★, Style ★★★★; affluent/active target; technical fabric cost |
| Children's Apparel | Family/value market with licensing upside | Value ★★★★, Durability ★★★★, Comfort ★★★★, IP ★★★★★; safety/size complexity |
| Handbag & Accessories | Small volume, high potential margin | Luxury ★★★★★, Style ★★★★★, Brand prestige ★★★★★; secure storage and counterfeiting risk |

### Segment preferences by product

| Segment | Best products | What they reward | What loses them |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Budget families | T-shirts, children's apparel, denim | Value, durability, multipacks, mass retail | Trend premiums and fragile materials |
| Young urban consumers | T-shirts, hoodies, activewear | Style, drops, sustainability, creator/IP collaborations, online channels | Slow collections and generic design |
| Middle-income adults | Denim, activewear, hoodies | Fit, comfort, durability, trusted brand | Poor sizing and trend-only propositions |
| Affluent fashion buyers | Activewear, handbags | Style, material quality, scarcity, prestige, flagship/DTC | Discount channels that dilute exclusivity |
| Families with children | Children's apparel, hoodies | Durability, comfort, value, beloved IP | Delicate products and weak retail availability |

### Required icons

`tshirt.svg`, `hoodie.svg`, `denim.svg`, `activewear.svg`, `kidsapparel.svg`,
`handbag.svg`. Use the 512×360 house style with one hero garment or neatly grouped outfit,
fabric folds and stitching visible, clean silhouette, soft shadow, no model faces and no text.

### Special building — Fashion Atelier

- **Gameplay:** unlocks AA apparel programs; higher levels increase collection-design capacity,
  trend-response speed and sample validation. It reduces late-season risk rather than adding a
  flat sales modifier.
- **Qualities:** trend forecasting, pattern cutting, fit testing, materials library, collection
  coherence and rapid sampling.
- **Map asset:** `fashion_atelier.png`, 768×768 RGBA isometric. Glass-front creative studio with
  colorful fabric rolls, cutting tables visible through windows, skylights, mannequin silhouettes
  and a small photo-shoot terrace. Contemporary violet/coral accents; no embedded words.

## 3. Consumer Electronics

### Strategic identity

High development cost, rapid obsolescence and meaningful defect/reputation risk. Performance and
reliability drive review scores, while ecosystem, ease of use and distribution determine adoption.
A strong specialist product can earn premium margin without mass-market volume.

Core priorities: **Performance, Reliability, Ease of Use, Design, Ecosystem, Value, Privacy**.

| Product | Commercial character | Strongest impacts |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Wireless Headphones | Broadest entry category | Sound/Performance ★★★★★, Comfort ★★★★, Design ★★★★, Value ★★★; online comparison pressure |
| Earbuds | High volume, compact and trend-sensitive | Convenience ★★★★★, Design ★★★★, Reliability ★★★★; battery/component cost; high competition |
| Smartwatch | Ecosystem and lifestyle product | Ecosystem ★★★★★, Ease ★★★★, Design ★★★★, Performance ★★★; affluent/active buyers |
| Gaming Controller | Enthusiast niche with IP potential | Performance ★★★★★, Reliability ★★★★★, Design ★★★★, IP ★★★★; premium niche margin |
| Smart Speaker | Household adoption and privacy tension | Ease ★★★★★, Ecosystem ★★★★★, Design ★★★, Privacy ★★★★; low standalone willingness to pay |
| Home Security Device | Trust-led recurring ecosystem | Reliability ★★★★★, Privacy ★★★★★, Ease ★★★★; affluent/family skew; service opportunity later |

### Segment preferences by product

| Segment | Best products | What they reward | What loses them |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Budget mass market | Headphones, earbuds | Value, acceptable reliability, marketplace availability | Premium features with weak brand trust |
| Young enthusiasts | Earbuds, controllers, smartwatches | Performance, design, novelty, gaming IP, online launch | Poor specs and conservative design |
| Affluent professionals | Headphones, smartwatches, security | Reliability, ecosystem, design, privacy, DTC service | Cheap presentation and fragmented software |
| Families/suburban homes | Speakers, security, headphones | Ease, reliability, compatibility, retail support | Complex setup and privacy concerns |
| Specialist gamers/creators | Controllers, headphones | Best-in-class performance, durability, relevant IP | Broad-market compromises; willing to pay more for a 4★+ review |

### Required icons

`headphones.svg`, `earbuds.svg`, `smartwatch.svg`, `controller.svg`, `smartspeaker.svg`,
`homesecurity.svg`. Use the same 512×360 SVG framing, precise but friendly shapes, recognizable
buttons/ports, restrained highlights, soft shadow, no real-world trademarks or interface text.

### Special building — Prototype & Reliability Lab

- **Gameplay:** unlocks AA electronics at level 1 and AAA at its final level alongside the global
  research gate. Improves prototype iteration speed and reliability validation; reduces defect and
  recall probability. It does not create automatic customer awareness.
- **Qualities:** hardware engineering, firmware integration, battery testing, drop/environmental
  testing, usability labs and failure analysis.
- **Map asset:** `electronics_prototype_lab.png`, 768×768 RGBA isometric. Blue-glass technical
  building with a visible prototype bench, compact anechoic chamber, rooftop solar/battery units,
  loading door and cyan status lighting. Same ground angle, shadow direction and visual density as
  the existing Research Center.

## Recommended implementation order

1. **Packaged Food** — maximizes reuse of value/IP/retail while proving shelf life and repeat purchase.
2. **Apparel** — adds fashion seasonality, collection speed and luxury-margin play.
3. **Consumer Electronics** — arrives last because reliability, obsolescence and higher project
   costs need the deepest balance work.

Each industry should ship first with six products, one special center, six SVG icons, two specialist
retailers, three competitors, material/BOM entries, storage/regulation rules and at least two
industry-specific events. That is enough to feel mechanically distinct without creating a separate
simulation engine for every category.
